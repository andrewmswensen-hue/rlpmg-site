How to read this table
  • Rent collected — gross rent less vacancy.
  • Mortgage — principal and interest only. It stops the year the loan is paid off.
  • Operating costs — taxes, insurance, HOA, management, leasing, maintenance, and the capital reserve.
  • Cash flow — what actually lands in your account that year, after income tax on the rental profit.
  • Wealth if you rent — what you'd walk away with if you rented through that year and then sold: sale price, less selling costs, less the loan payoff, less capital gains and depreciation recapture tax, plus every dollar of cash flow you banked along the way.
  • Wealth if you sell now — today's net proceeds invested at your assumed return through that year.

Both columns are measured the same way: cash in hand, after tax, after the cost of selling. That makes them directly comparable in any year, not just the last one.

How this calculator works, and where it stops. It amortizes your actual loan balance month by month, applies straight-line depreciation over 27.5 years on the building portion of your basis, carries suspended passive losses forward and releases them at sale, expires the Section 121 exclusion three years after you move out, and taxes depreciation recapture separately from capital gains. Both scenarios are compared on the same footing: after tax, after the cost of selling, with idle cash on both sides earning the same return.
  • It does not model a 1031 exchange, cost segregation, the net investment income tax, AMT, or the qualified business income deduction.
  • Passive loss rules can defer deductions in ways that depend on your income; this model releases them at sale.
  • Appreciation, rent growth, and investment returns are assumptions, not forecasts. Change them and watch the answer move.

This is an estimate for planning, not tax or investment advice. Run the numbers by your CPA before you decide. RL Property Management is a licensed Ohio real estate brokerage.
