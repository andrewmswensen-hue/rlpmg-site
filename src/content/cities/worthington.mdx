## What is it like to own a rental in Worthington?

Worthington is a city in Franklin County, roughly 12 miles north of downtown Columbus, about a 20-minute drive. Population is approximately 14,786. Local anchors include Worthington Village Green and the Old Worthington historic core, St. John's Episcopal Church (1817), The brick building at 679-681 High Street, described as the oldest commercial building in continuous use in Ohio, and Orange Johnson House and the Worthington Historical Society.

## What should an owner know about the Worthington rental market?

Small, historic, fully built-out northern suburb founded in 1803, effectively landlocked by Columbus. Two things drive management here. First, Worthington Schools (Thomas Worthington and Worthington Kilbourne high schools) is the rent driver, and the district reaches beyond city limits into parts of Columbus, so a Worthington schools address and a Worthington city address are not the same thing, which affects both rent and which ordinances apply. Second, the Architectural Review District: any exterior change in Old Worthington needs a Certificate of Appropriateness from the seven-member Architectural Review Board under Chapter 1177, so windows, siding, roofing, fences and signage all require lead time. Worthington adopted a source-of-income ordinance in 2021. Housing stock ranges from genuine early-1800s buildings downtown to large 1950s-1960s Colonial Hills tracts.

Sources and detail:

- Ohio Revised Code 5323.02 requires owners of residential rental property in counties over 200,000 residents (which includes Franklin County) to file owner and contact information with the county auditor. Registration is one-time and free, must be filed within 60 days of transfer or of any change, and non-compliance carries a $150 per-property, per-tax-year fine.
