## What is it like to own a rental in German Village?

German Village is a Columbus neighborhood in Franklin County, roughly 1 mile south-southeast of downtown Columbus. Local landmarks and anchors include Schiller Park (23 acres, with the Actors' Theatre amphitheater), The Book Loft (32-room bookstore), St. Mary of the Assumption Catholic Church (1865), and Stewart Alternative Elementary School (1874). The single most regulation-sensitive rental submarket in Columbus. It is the largest privately funded historic district on the National Register, and the German Village Commission has approval authority over any exterior change, an owner cannot swap windows, replace a roof, repaint brick or install a fence without going through the Commission. Budget extra time and cost on each turn and each capital item, and do not let a contractor start exterior work without checking approval status. Housing stock is mid-1800s red brick on brick-paved streets, so foundations, knob-and-tube wiring, and lead paint are routine findings. Tenant demand is strong and rents are premium: walkable to downtown, the Brewery District and Schiller Park, drawing professionals and empty nesters rather than families. Parking is scarce and is a real leasing factor. As part of Columbus, the source-of-income ordinance and the Chapter 598 short-term-rental permit rules apply.
