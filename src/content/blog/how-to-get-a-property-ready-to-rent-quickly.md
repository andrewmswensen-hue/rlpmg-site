This is why keeping your units occupied is a top priority, it ensures ongoing income from the property.

The longer it sits vacant, the more revenue opportunities you're losing.

That said, it's worth spending the time and money it takes to adequately prepare your unit for rent because a nicely outfitted rental unit will be able to command a higher rental rate in your market in addition to attracting better tenants.

The good news is you can still move through this process quickly and thoroughly by following a turnover checklist.
<h2><strong>Your Ready-to-Rent Checklist</strong></h2>
Tenants are savvy and they're not going to rent from just any place. In fact, you want your tenant to be picky. That's a good indicator they will be respectful of your property and keep it in good shape.

As you prepare your property for rent, knowing what to prioritize during a <a href="/blog/how-do-you-turn-over-a-rental/"><u>rental turnover</u></a> is step one. Step two is creating a repeatable workflow you can follow again and again each time you need to get a property ready to rent. This will not only make you more efficient, but it will also ensure that you don't miss anything.

Here are some essentials you need to include on your list:
<ul>
 	<li><strong>Do a deep clean</strong> - Whether this is the first time you're renting out the unit or you are between tenants, it's essential to do a deep, deep clean of the property. Your deep clean should include all the nooks, crannies, cracks and crevices of the unit, and especially the areas that don't often get touched. By setting a standard of cleanliness, you're also demonstrating the condition you expect the tenants to keep the place.</li>
 	<li><strong>Tackle repairs and upgrades</strong> - This might include adding fresh paint, installing new carpets, upgrading appliances (or at a minimum conducting maintenance checks), fixing leaks and ensuring that all systems are running safely and efficiently.</li>
</ul>
<ul>
 	<li><strong>Make aesthetic improvements</strong> - Other simple and inexpensive upgrades can also improve the overall unit's appeal. Things like improving lighting and focusing on finishes can be a cost-effective way to improve the unit. And don't forget about the unit's exterior. Single-family homes should have nice landscaping, trimmed shrubs and be free of debris.</li>
</ul>
The last step in the turnover process is to list your unit for rent. By doing a thorough job when you prepare your rental unit, you should be able to secure a high-quality tenant who will stay in your unit for years to come.
