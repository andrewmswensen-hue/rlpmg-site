<h2><b>Recognizing When Eviction Is the Right Step</b></h2>
<a href="https://youtu.be/TwR-DQf7vtQ?si=F_JuqbM9tNZM3ZQx" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Evictions</a> are serious, and Ohio law only permits them under specific circumstances. Jumping the gun or acting on emotion can land landlords in legal hot water. Before starting the process, make sure your situation qualifies as a lawful reason for eviction in Columbus.

&nbsp;
<h3><b>Legally Valid Reasons to Evict a Tenant in Ohio</b></h3>
The most common legal grounds for eviction include:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Non-payment of rent</b>, Even one missed month can trigger an eviction notice if not resolved</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><a href="https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/chapter-5321" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><b>Lease violations</b></a>, This includes unauthorized pets, additional tenants not listed on the lease, or intentional damage to the unit.</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Failure to vacate after the lease ends</b>, Known as a "holdover" tenant, this situation arises when someone stays without renewing.</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Illegal activity</b>, Drug use, violence, or criminal behavior on the property are all grounds for immediate action.</li>
</ul>
Each of these reasons must be clearly documented and communicated before proceeding.

&nbsp;
<h3><b>Early Warning Signs That Shouldn't Be Ignored</b></h3>
Often, issues build up long before they hit the point of eviction. Pay attention to early red flags such as:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Missed or partial rent payments</b>, Keep a detailed rent ledger and note all payment dates</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Tenant complaints or neighbor reports</b>, These can indicate recurring noise, trash, or safety issues.</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Avoidance or lack of response</b>, If a tenant stops replying to emails, ignores written notices, or refuses entry for inspections, that's a major concern</li>
</ul>
&nbsp;
<h3><b>Be Proactive, Not Reactive</b></h3>
The best way to avoid a messy eviction is to stay ahead of problems. That means:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1">Keeping detailed records of all communications and payments</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">Addressing issues as soon as they arise</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">Sending written warnings when a lease violation is discovered</li>
</ul>
Eviction should never be the first option, but when needed, it must be done by the book. Professional property management can help you spot problems early and handle tenant issues before they escalate.

<h2><b>The Ohio Eviction Process, Step-by-Step</b></h2>
Once you've confirmed that eviction is legally justified, the next step is to follow the formal process outlined by Ohio law. Skipping or mishandling even one step can delay your case, or worse, get it dismissed. Here's how the eviction process works for landlords in Columbus.
<h3><b>Step 1: Issue a 3-Day Notice to Vacate</b></h3>
Before anything can be filed in court, <a href="https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/chapter-1923" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Ohio law</a> generally requires that you serve the tenant with a <b>3-Day Notice to Vacate</b>.
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Delivery method</b>: Must be <a href="https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-1923.04" target="_blank" rel="noopener">hand-delivered</a> to the tenant or clearly posted on the door</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><a href="https://www.ohiolegalhelp.org/eviction-timeline" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><b>Timeline</b></a>: The notice gives the tenant 3 business days to either pay in full or move out</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Documentation</b>: Always include a rent ledger, total balance owed, and clear payment instructions</li>
</ul>
If the tenant pays in full within those 3 business days, the eviction process stops. If not, you can proceed to court.

&nbsp;
<h3><b>Step 2: File the Eviction Complaint in Franklin County</b></h3>
After the 3-day notice expires with no resolution, you'll file an <b>eviction complaint</b> with the <a href="https://municipalcourt.franklincountyohio.gov/Courts/Eviction-Court/Filing-an-Eviction-What-you-need-to-know" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Franklin County Municipal Court.</a>
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Wait period</b>: File on the fourth business day after serving the notice</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Where to file</b>: Franklin County Environmental and Housing Division</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Typical fees may be approximately</b>:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="2">$123 court filing fee</li>
 	<li aria-level="2">$150 attorney fee (if using legal counsel)</li>
 	<li aria-level="2">Possible service fees or document preparation charges</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
Once filed, the court will set a hearing date, usually within 2–3 weeks.

&nbsp;
<h3><b>Step 3: Attend the Court Hearing</b></h3>
You or your attorney will attend the hearing to present your case.
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Prepare documentation</b>:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="2">Signed lease agreement</li>
 	<li aria-level="2">Payment records</li>
 	<li aria-level="2">Copies of all notices and communications</li>
</ul>
</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Outcome</b>: In many cases, when documentation is complete and valid, courts may rule in the landlord's favor, but outcomes vary.</li>
</ul>
A judgment will be issued, and the tenant is ordered to vacate by a certain date.

&nbsp;
<h3><b>Step 4: Schedule a Set-Out (If Necessary)</b></h3>
If the tenant still refuses to leave, you'll need to coordinate a formal removal process, known as a <b>set-out</b>.
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>The court issues a writ of restitution</b>, authorizing the bailiff to proceed.</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Coordinate vendors</b>:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="2">A licensed locksmith to change locks</li>
 	<li aria-level="2">A clean-out crew to remove belongings</li>
 	<li aria-level="2">The Franklin County bailiff is to supervise</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
&nbsp;

<b>Additional costs may include</b>:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1">$80 Tag and Set-Out Fee (<a href="https://municipalcourt.franklincountyohio.gov/Courts/Eviction-Court" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Franklin County</a>)</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">For clients, RLPM offers an optional $250 coordination service to handle this process (if using professional property management)</li>
</ul>
At this stage, you regain legal possession of the unit and can begin the process of turning it over to the next tenant.

<h2><b>Common Eviction Mistakes to Avoid</b></h2>
Even experienced landlords can make costly mistakes when handling an eviction. Ohio's eviction laws are specific, and missteps can easily lead to delays, dismissals, or legal trouble. Here are some of the most common issues, and how to steer clear of them.

&nbsp;
<h3><b>1. Improper Notice or Incomplete Paperwork</b></h3>
One of the most frequent errors is serving a <b>notice with incorrect dates</b> or missing legally required information. Common missteps include:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1">Using outdated or informal notice forms</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">Failing to list the correct number of days or the amount due</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">Skipping the rent ledger or payment instructions</li>
</ul>
Any of these can result in the court dismissing your case entirely, forcing you to restart the process.

&nbsp;
<h3><b>2. Missing Deadlines or Court Dates</b></h3>
Eviction cases are time-sensitive. If you:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>File too early</b> after serving the notice,</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Miss your hearing</b>, or</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Fail to bring proper documentation</b>,</li>
</ul>
You risk losing the case or having it postponed. Judges often favor tenants when landlords appear unprepared.

&nbsp;
<h3><b>3. Violating Fair Housing or Retaliation Laws</b></h3>
You cannot evict a tenant in response to:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1">Filing a complaint against you</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">Requesting repairs</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">Reporting code violations</li>
</ul>
Doing so can result in counterclaims, legal penalties, or damage to your reputation. Always document lease violations clearly and avoid emotional decisions.

&nbsp;
<h3><b>4. Not Planning for Post-Eviction Clean-Up</b></h3>
Many landlords underestimate how much time and money it takes to prepare a unit after a forced move-out. Issues may include:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1">Trash left behind</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">Wall and flooring damage</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">Pest infestations or biohazards</li>
</ul>
Document everything with photos immediately, and don't delay the turnover process.

<ul>
