<h2><strong>Inflation and Its Impact on Real Estate Investments</strong></h2>
<p>Investments of any kind carry risk, but there are certain investments that are less risky than others. While their returns may not be as high, neither are their potential losses.</p>
<p>Investing in real estate properties is one such investment opportunity that can help you balance out other riskier items in your portfolio. A few reasons this is the case:</p>

<h2><strong>Real Estate has Fixed Rate Debt</strong></h2>
<p>Obviously not all debt is fixed rate, but for the most part it is readily available for investors. So if you lock in your costs at a fixed rate for a longer duration, you are benefitting from inflation rather than being hurt by it.</p>

<h2><strong>The Ability to Increase Cash Flow With Fixed Costs</strong></h2>
<p>One of the biggest misnomers in real estate is that rents rise with inflation. In short, they don't, the market determines how much an apartment can rent for. The market <em>can</em> be influenced by inflation in a way so that rents go up, but inflation doesn't cause rents to go up.</p>
<p>This being said, there are multiple ways to increase net operating income (NOI) to increase cash flow, remodels that can increase rents (if you don't have laundry in units, this can be a huge value add), decreasing water usage, requoting insurance, among others.</p>

<h2><strong>Getting Good Long Term Returns in Real Estate</strong></h2>
<p>As an asset class, real estate consistently performs well, even during economic recessions. Nothing is guaranteed though, and more than one real estate empire has been brought down by heavy amounts of debt and declining market rates for rent.</p>
<p>At the end of the day, looking for long term results is a balance of good cash flow today and price appreciation tomorrow.
