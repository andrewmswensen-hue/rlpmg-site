<h2><b>Your First (and Best) Line of Defense: Why Screening Matters More Than Ever</b></h2>
Tenant screening is more than just a formality. In today's <b>fast-moving Columbus rental market</b>, it's a critical defense against late rent, property damage, and legal trouble. Applicants are applying for multiple units at once, often pressuring landlords to make quick decisions.

<b>Skipping proper screening is one of the top regrets we hear from new clients.</b> It's easy to fall for a well-dressed, friendly applicant who says all the right things. But without a structured review process, even the savviest investors risk choosing poorly.

Bad tenants can lead to:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Lost rent</b> from late or missed payments</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Property damage</b> that costs thousands to repair</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Legal liability</b> for improper denials or Fair Housing violations</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Stress</b> from constant conflict, repairs, or neighbor complaints</li>
</ul>
The good news? You can prevent most of these problems with consistent, criteria-based screening, the same kind we use at RL Property Management.

<h2><b>Red Flags in Tenant Applications: What to Watch For</b></h2>
Every application tells a story. And while no single issue is disqualifying, patterns matter. Here are some of the biggest <b>red flags in tenant screening</b>:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Inconsistent income</b>: One pay stub isn't enough. Ask for 2–3 months of pay history and verify with a direct call to their employer.</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Unverifiable employment</b>: HR numbers are often black holes. Instead, ask for a direct supervisor or manager's contact to confirm the details.</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Frequent moves</b>: Look beyond the dates. If they've lived in 4 places in 3 years, that's a potential sign of instability.</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Past evictions or legal judgments</b>: Even dismissed evictions can signal chronic issues. Always check the full rental history.</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Pressure tactics or urgency</b>: "I can pay right now if you approve me today" can be a red flag. Good tenants understand a proper process.</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Incomplete applications</b>: Missing phone numbers, vague landlord details, or skipped sections should make you pause.</li>
</ul>
Each of these warning signs may be explainable, but none should be ignored. Always dig deeper.

<h2><b>Red Flags During the Tenant Interview</b></h2>
Application checks are just the beginning. When speaking to a potential tenant, in person or on the phone, listen for these subtle signs:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1">They give <b>contradictory answers</b> that don't match the application.</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">They overshare <b>personal sob stories</b> to gain sympathy and skip the line.</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">They are <b>evasive</b> or vague when asked about previous landlords.</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">They <b>resist background checks</b> or try to redirect the conversation.</li>
</ul>
A great interview doesn't guarantee a great tenant. But a clumsy or overly emotional one should prompt extra scrutiny.

<h2><b>References: What to Trust, What to Question</b></h2>
Many landlords rely too heavily on references, assuming they're accurate. But smart screening means verifying, not just listening.

When checking landlord references:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Cross-check property addresses</b> against county records to ensure it's not a friend pretending to be a landlord.</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Verify ownership</b> through LLC names or tax records.</li>
</ul>
For employers:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1">Watch out for <b>vague job titles</b> or unusually short tenures.</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">Be wary of overly enthusiastic responses, they might be scripted.</li>
</ul>
<b>Pro Tip:</b> Ask this one question to trip up fake references: <i>"Can you confirm the move-out date for that property?"</i> If they hesitate or guess, that's a red flag.

<h2><b>What If You Need to Say No?</b></h2>
Sometimes, the best decision is to deny an application. But even that requires care.
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1">Always issue a <b>written denial letter</b>, especially if credit is involved (required under the Fair Credit Reporting Act).</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">Document the reason clearly and make sure it's based on your <b>published screening criteria</b>.</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">Avoid language that could be misinterpreted and stick to facts.</li>
</ul>
In a high-scrutiny market like Columbus, <b>Fair Housing compliance</b> isn't optional. Inconsistent or undocumented processes can lead to legal trouble fast.
