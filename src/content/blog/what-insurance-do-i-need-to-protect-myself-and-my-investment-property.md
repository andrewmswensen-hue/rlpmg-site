For property investors, wading through the numerous insurance coverage offerings and policies can feel especially overwhelming, but it's imperative to protect yourself against property losses, claims from a tenant, or potential lawsuit costs.</p>
<p>In this post, we'll discuss several insurance options you should consider to best protect yourself and your investment. Before deciding on a specific policy, it's also a good idea to turn to a professional insurance broker who can offer input specific to your business and personal interests.</p>

<h2><strong>Types of Insurance for Property Owners</strong></h2>
<p>First off, from a risk and liability perspective, it's generally advisable to own your rental investment properties as a business entity or LLC. In this way, you can keep your personal and LLC assets completely separate and add a layer of personal protection.</p>
<p>When investing in property, these are some commonly recommended types of insurance policies to keep active, in addition to your homeowner's policy.</p>

<ul>
 	<li><strong>Liability insurance</strong> - This helps protect you if someone is accidentally injured on your property, be that tenants, guests or anyone else who comes to your property.</li>
</ul>
<ul>
 	<li><strong>Hazard and fire insurance </strong>- Typically this is included in a basic property dwelling insurance policy and covers the replacement cost of the property if there was damage due to a hazard.</li>
 	<li><strong>Loss of income coverage </strong>- If your property is uninhabitable due for an extended period of time to a covered loss, loss of rent coverage helps recoup some of that income by reimbursing you with the fair rental value of the dwelling during that time period.</li>
 	<li><strong>Umbrella insurance</strong> - This is an additional type of coverage that offers protection beyond the limits and coverages of your other policies.</li>
</ul>
<p>In addition to insurance policies for you and your business, another important way to protect yourself and your investment property is to require your tenants to have a renter's insurance policy. Renter's insurance protects a tenant's personal property in the event of a fire, theft or other disaster. It also reduces the property owner's liability.</p>
