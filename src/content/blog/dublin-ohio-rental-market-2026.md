<h2 id="why-dublin">Why Dublin Commands Premium Rents</h2>
Dublin sits northwest of Columbus, straddling Franklin and Delaware counties, with a <a href="https://www.niche.com/places-to-live/dublin-franklin-oh/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">population of approximately 49,000</a>. It's the largest suburb in RLPM's service area and consistently ranks among the top places to live in Ohio: Niche gives it an overall A+ livability grade and ranks it #6 among best places to live in the Columbus metro.

What separates Dublin from other high-performing Columbus suburbs is scale. This isn't a small, quiet bedroom community. Dublin is home to more than <a href="https://dublinohiousa.gov/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">20 corporate headquarters</a>, 4,300+ businesses, and the <strong>Bridge Street District</strong>, a walkable, mixed-use urban center that has drawn national recognition for transforming a suburban landscape into something that looks and functions more like a small city. The <strong>Bridge Park</strong> development within that district combines Class A office, residential, retail, dining, and entertainment within walking distance, drawing the kind of professional tenants that investors want.

Dublin also carries a national reputation for safety. It's consistently named one of the safest cities in Ohio, with violent crime rates roughly 2.7 times below the state average and 3.3 times below the national average, according to <a href="https://www.areavibes.com/dublin-oh/crime/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">AreaVibes</a>. For families weighing suburban options, the combination of top-ranked schools, corporate employment access, and low crime makes Dublin the first name on the list.

<p><strong>Dublin isn't just a suburb. It's a self-contained economic engine with 20+ corporate headquarters and the walkable infrastructure to retain premium tenants.</strong></p>
</blockquote>

<h2 id="home-values">Home Values and Acquisition Costs</h2>
Dublin is the highest-priced major suburb in RLPM's service area. The <a href="https://www.zillow.com/home-values/4426/dublin-oh/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Zillow Home Value Index</a> places the typical Dublin home at approximately $545,900, up 3.5% over the past year. <a href="https://www.redfin.com/city/5847/OH/Dublin/housing-market" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Redfin reports a recent median sale price of $580,000</a>, with homes receiving an average of 3 offers and spending about 89 days on market.

The acquisition cost is the main consideration for investors. At a median purchase near $580,000 and average SFR rents around $3,000–$3,300/month, gross rental yield falls in the range of 6.2–6.8%. That's competitive but not exceptional on paper. The real return in Dublin comes from two places: appreciation (3.5% year-over-year, among the strongest in the metro) and tenant quality (longer tenures, fewer turnovers, lower maintenance costs relative to income).

Investors should also note that Dublin's housing stock skews newer than many Columbus suburbs. About 34% of rental buildings were <a href="https://www.rentcafe.com/average-rent-market-trends/us/oh/dublin/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">built between 1990 and 1999</a>, and 22% were built between 2010 and 2019. That means fewer surprise capital expenses from aging systems compared to older neighborhoods like Clintonville or parts of Westerville.
<h2></h2>
<h2 id="schools">Schools: The #1 District in Columbus</h2>
<a href="https://www.niche.com/k12/d/dublin-city-schools-oh/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Dublin City Schools</a> is the top-ranked school district in the Columbus metro area on Niche, with an overall A+ grade. The district ranks #4 in all of Ohio and #128 nationally. Those aren't vanity metrics: 73% of students score proficient or above in math, and 77% in reading, both significantly above the state averages of 55% and 60%, respectively.

The district serves approximately 16,736 students across 24 schools. Niche also ranks Dublin City Schools #2 in Ohio for best teachers and #2 for best places to teach, which speaks to faculty retention and the resulting stability for families.

For rental investors, the school district is the single most important demand driver. 64% of Dublin renters hold a bachelor's degree or higher. 55% of rental households are families, and 33% include children under 18. These are tenants who chose Dublin specifically for the schools, and they don't leave until their kids graduate. That translates directly to longer lease terms and lower turnover.

<h2></h2>
<h2 id="employers">Employers, Corporate Headquarters, and Growth Pipeline</h2>
Dublin's economic base is unusually strong for a suburb of its size. The city is home to more than <a href="https://dublinohiousa.gov/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">20 corporate headquarters</a> and 4,300+ businesses. Major employers include <strong>Cardinal Health</strong> (a Fortune 15 company), <strong>Wendy's International</strong> (global headquarters), <strong>IGS Energy</strong>, <strong>Stanley Steemer</strong>, <strong>OCLC</strong>, and <strong>Ashland Inc.</strong>, among others.

This corporate density creates a self-reinforcing cycle for rental demand: companies locate in Dublin for the talent pool and quality of life, employees rent in Dublin for the short commute and schools, and property values appreciate because both supply and demand are structurally constrained.

Nine Dublin-based companies earned spots on <a href="https://www.econdev.dublinohiousa.gov/building-momentum-dublins-2025-economic-development-achievements-and-2026-vision/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Columbus Business First's 2025 Fast 50 List</a> (fastest-growing private companies in the Columbus region), signaling that the employer base is expanding, not just maintaining.

The broader Columbus region's growth story also benefits Dublin directly. The <strong>Intel semiconductor facility</strong> in nearby Licking County and the associated supply chain buildout are expected to generate thousands of jobs requiring proximity to the northwest corridor. Dublin's combination of housing, schools, and highway access (I-270, US-33, SR-161) makes it a natural landing spot for relocating professionals.
<h2></h2>
<h2 id="development">Bridge Park, Bridge North, and What's Coming Next</h2>
Dublin's growth story isn't just about what exists today. Three major development initiatives are shaping the next 5–10 years of rental demand:
<h3>Bridge North (breaking ground 2026)</h3>
<a href="https://dublinohiousa.gov/city-news/dublin-city-council-approves-development-agreement-for-bridge-north-development-increasing-office-space-and-workforce-in-bridge-street-district/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Bridge North</a> is a mixed-use project on Riverside Drive that includes 75,000 sq ft of new Class A office space, 280 multi-family residential units, retail space, structured parking, and a 150-key Tempo by Hilton hotel. Construction is expected to begin in 2026. This project extends the Bridge Park model northward and will bring additional professional tenants into the Dublin rental market.
<h3>West Innovation District</h3>
The city acquired <a href="https://www.econdev.dublinohiousa.gov/building-momentum-dublins-2025-economic-development-achievements-and-2026-vision/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">349 additional acres along the SR-161 corridor</a> in 2026 for the West Innovation District. Combined with proximity to Ohio University's Dublin campus and the 33 Smart Mobility Corridor, this district is positioned for enterprise campus development. Infrastructure planning is underway, with the integrated implementation strategy being finalized.
<h3>Metro Center Revitalization</h3>
The 210-acre Metro Center district is being transformed into a walkable, mixed-use neighborhood with modern office and commercial spaces connected to 100-gig fiber. City Council adopted the revitalization plan in late 2024, and acceleration is expected through 2026.

For investors, these projects represent a structural tailwind: more jobs, more professionals, and more demand for rental housing in a market where supply is already constrained.
<blockquote class="post-pullquote">
<p><strong>Bridge North alone adds 280 residential units and 75,000 sq ft of office. The West Innovation District and Metro Center are next.</strong></p>
</blockquote>

<h2 id="vacancy-timing">Vacancy and Lease Timing</h2>
Dublin's rental supply is structurally limited. Only <a href="https://www.rentcafe.com/average-rent-market-trends/us/oh/dublin/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">23% of Dublin's housing is renter-occupied</a> (77% homeowners), which constrains available inventory. When well-priced rental properties hit the market, they tend to lease quickly, especially during peak season.

Seasonal leasing in Dublin follows the Columbus metro pattern: strongest demand from May through September, driven by family relocations timed to the school year and corporate start dates. Properties listed during the spring and summer lease-up window command the strongest rents.

The tenant profile in Dublin skews toward longer stays. With 55% of renter households being families (many with school-age children), lease renewals are common and turnover is lower than in more transient Columbus neighborhoods. The median renter tenure in Dublin is approximately 2018 (per Census data), suggesting multi-year stays are the norm.

<h2 id="investor-considerations">What Investors Should Know Before Buying in Dublin</h2>
<h3>Higher acquisition cost, higher quality tenant</h3>
Dublin's median home price ($546,000–$580,000) is the highest in RLPM's service area. That means more capital deployed per unit. The offsetting factors: premium rents ($3,000+/month for SFR), stronger appreciation (3.5% YOY), longer tenant tenures, and lower turnover costs. For investors who can clear the acquisition threshold, the total return profile is strong.
<h3>New construction competition</h3>
Dublin has a significant and growing inventory of modern apartment communities, particularly in the Bridge Street District. Single-family investors compete on space, privacy, yards, and garages. Families with children in Dublin City Schools are the sweet spot: they want a house, not an apartment, and they're willing to pay $3,000+/month to get one.
<h3>School district boundaries matter</h3>
Not every property in the Dublin zip code falls within Dublin City Schools. Some areas are served by Hilliard City Schools or other districts. The school district is the primary demand driver for family renters, so confirming that a property sits within Dublin City Schools boundaries before purchasing is essential.
<h3>Delaware County vs. Franklin County</h3>
Dublin straddles two counties. Properties in the Delaware County portion may have different tax rates, zoning rules, and assessment schedules than those in Franklin County. Investors should factor county-specific property tax rates into their cash flow projections.
<blockquote class="post-pullquote">
<p><strong>Confirm school district boundaries before buying. A Dublin address doesn't always mean Dublin City Schools, and that distinction drives rent.</strong></p>
</blockquote>

<h2 id="faq">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<strong>Is Dublin, Ohio a good place to invest in rental property?</strong>
Dublin offers the strongest school district, deepest corporate employer base, and highest rents of any suburb in the Columbus metro. The higher acquisition cost limits competition from casual investors, and tenant quality is among the best you'll find. It's well-suited for investors prioritizing long-term appreciation and stability over maximum yield.

<strong>What do rentals go for in Dublin, Ohio?</strong>
Apartments average around $1,586/month across all sizes. Single-family homes rent for an average of $3,000–$3,300+/month depending on size, location within the district, and condition.

<strong>What school district is Dublin, Ohio in?</strong>
Dublin is primarily served by Dublin City Schools, which holds an A+ grade and the #1 ranking in the Columbus area on Niche. Some Dublin addresses fall within Hilliard City Schools, so investors should verify district boundaries before purchasing.

<strong>What is the median home price in Dublin, Ohio?</strong>
As of early 2026, the Zillow Home Value Index places the typical Dublin home at approximately $546,000. Redfin reports a recent median sale price of $580,000.

<strong>What major companies are headquartered in Dublin?</strong>
Dublin is home to 20+ corporate headquarters including Cardinal Health (Fortune 15), Wendy's International, IGS Energy, Stanley Steemer, OCLC, and Ashland Inc. The city hosts 4,300+ total businesses.

<strong>How does Dublin compare to Westerville or Upper Arlington for rental investment?</strong>
Dublin commands higher rents and home values than both. It offers the #1 school district (vs. A-minus for Westerville and Upper Arlington), stronger corporate employer density, and more active commercial development. The tradeoff is a higher acquisition cost, which means more capital per door but stronger tenant stability and appreciation.

<strong>What is Bridge Park and how does it affect the rental market?</strong>
Bridge Park is Dublin's flagship mixed-use development in the Bridge Street District, combining office, residential, retail, and dining in a walkable urban format. It draws corporate tenants and professionals who want an urban lifestyle within a suburban school district. Bridge North, breaking ground in 2026, extends this model with 280 new residential units and 75,000 sq ft of office.

<strong>Is Dublin safe?</strong>
Dublin earns an A+ livability grade on Niche and is consistently ranked among the safest cities in Ohio. Violent crime rates are roughly 2.7 times below the state average and 3.3 times below the national average.
