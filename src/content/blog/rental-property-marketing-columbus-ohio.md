In Columbus, the summer months bring a rush of tenant movement. But while the demand is high, so is the competition. If your property isn't marketed well, the best renters will scroll right past it, or worse, never see it at all.

High-quality tenants don't just stumble onto a great rental. They respond to properties that look professional, feel well-managed, and are clearly a good fit. That means your marketing strategy needs to be intentional, polished, and targeted.

Let's walk through the exact steps that lead to <b>better tenants, fewer headaches, and stronger rental income</b>, starting with who you actually want to attract.

&nbsp;
<h2>Know Who You're Marketing To: Define Your Ideal Tenant</h2>
Not every applicant is the right fit for your property (and that's a good thing). The best marketing starts with clarity: <b>Who do you actually want living in your rental?</b>

Your <b>ideal tenant</b> isn't just someone who pays rent on time (though that's a must). It's someone who matches your property's value, lease expectations, and maintenance standards. Think about:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Rent Price</b>, Higher rents attract different demographics than budget options. If your property is priced for $1,800/month, your ideal tenant likely has stable income, a solid credit history, and expects a certain level of quality.
</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Lease Term</b>, Looking for long-term renters? Then your marketing should appeal to tenants seeking stability,  not those in transition or between life stages.
</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Lifestyle &amp; Fit</b>, A quiet residential duplex in a school district attracts a different audience than a trendy downtown loft. Tailoring your messaging helps set expectations upfront.
</li>
</ul>
<h3>Property Managers Use Personas for a Reason</h3>
Professional property managers, like RLPM, use <b>tenant personas</b> to guide every listing. These aren't random guesses,  they're data-informed profiles that help match the right renters to the right properties. For example:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1">A two-bedroom near Polaris might target dual-income professionals.&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">A three-bedroom in Dublin could be ideal for a small family with school-aged kids.&nbsp;</li>
</ul>
By aligning your listing with the right tenant profile, you naturally filter out mismatches and attract applicants who are more likely to <b>stay longer, pay on time, and treat the property well</b>.

Think of this step as the foundation. The clearer your picture of the ideal tenant, the easier it becomes to craft marketing that speaks directly to them, and only them.

&nbsp;
<h2>Make Your Listing Pop: The Basics That Actually Attract Great Tenants</h2>
The listing is your first impression, and high-quality tenants are judging. Fast. You have just seconds to grab attention and make someone say, "I want to live there." Here's how to make every element count.
<h3>1. Start With Stunning Photos</h3>
<b>Photos sell the property. Period.</b> If your images are dark, cluttered, or taken at odd angles, your ideal renter is gone before they even read the details.

What works:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Natural Light</b>, Shoot during the day when sunlight floods the space. Open blinds. Turn on the lights.&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Wide Angles</b>, Use a wide-angle lens (or hire someone who can) to make rooms feel spacious and welcoming.&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Clean and Staged</b>, Tidy up. A clean, empty (or lightly staged) space always shows better. Even just removing small clutter makes a difference.&nbsp;</li>
</ul>
RLPM tip: We use professional photographers for every listing. That's because quality images lead to <b>more clicks, more showings, and faster leasing.</b>
<h3>2. Headlines That Work Hard</h3>
A good headline does two things: <b>grabs attention and sets expectations.</b>

Skip the boring "2BR/1BA Apartment" format. Instead, write something like:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1">"Updated Clintonville 2BR with Garage + Private Yard"&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">"Modern 1-Bedroom Near OSU, Walk to Dining &amp; Transit"&nbsp;</li>
</ul>
Be specific. Paint a quick picture of what makes the unit special.
<h3>3. Descriptions That Sell</h3>
Your description should highlight:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Key features</b> (washer/dryer, updated kitchen, garage)&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Amenities</b> (central air, fenced yard, pet-friendly)&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Location benefits</b> (near top-rated schools, close to downtown, quiet cul-de-sac)&nbsp;</li>
</ul>
Also include <b>minimum requirements</b> like income standards, background check policies, and lease terms. This filters out unqualified leads before they even reach out.
<h3>4. Use Searchable, Smart Keywords</h3>
Most renters search by location and must-haves. Be sure to include phrases like:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1">"rental near Polaris Columbus Ohio"&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">"close to Westerville schools"&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">"2-bedroom apartment with parking in Dublin"&nbsp;</li>
</ul>
Using the right keywords doesn't just help people find your listing,  it helps the <i>right</i> people find it.

&nbsp;
<h2>Get It Seen: Where (and Where Not) to Advertise Your Rental</h2>
You can write the best listing in the world, but if no one sees it, it won't matter. Strategic advertising is what connects your rental to the right audience. Here's how to make sure your listing gets real exposure - not just eyeballs, but clicks from qualified renters.
<h3>The Big Players: Zillow, Apartments.com, and Facebook Marketplace</h3>
Each platform has strengths and drawbacks:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Zillow Rental Network</b>&nbsp;
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="2"><i>Pros:</i> Huge traffic, renter-friendly interface, high visibility.&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="2"><i>Cons:</i> Listings now cost money and may prioritize premium users.&nbsp;</li>
</ul>
</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Apartments.com (CoStar Group)</b>&nbsp;
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="2"><i>Pros:</i> Syndicates across multiple sites like ApartmentFinder and ForRent.&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="2"><i>Cons:</i> Often geared toward larger multi-family buildings, single-family homes can get buried.&nbsp;</li>
</ul>
</li>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Facebook Marketplace</b>&nbsp;
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="2"><i>Pros:</i> Free, fast exposure, especially for mid-priced and affordable rentals.&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="2"><i>Cons:</i> Flooded with scams, fake accounts, and time-wasting inquiries. Limited search filters.&nbsp;</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<h3>Syndicated Listing Networks vs. Going DIY</h3>

<h2>Timing Is Everything: Why When You List Matters</h2>
The real estate saying "location, location, location" might be famous, but when it comes to <b>rental success</b>, <b>timing</b> is just as important.
<h3>Summer = Peak Tenant Movement</h3>
In Columbus, Ohio, the rental market heats up from <b>May through August</b>. School's out, leases are ending, and people are planning moves before the new school year or job cycle begins. That means:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1">More renters are actively looking&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">More qualified applications come in quickly&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">Renters are more likely to sign longer leases&nbsp;</li>
</ul>
List during this window, and your odds of finding a reliable, long-term tenant go way up.
<h3>Avoiding the Vacancy Gap</h3>
Every day your unit sits empty is money lost. Worse? Poor timing can cause that vacancy to stretch for <b>weeks or even months</b>.

Here's what happens when you list at the wrong time:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1"><b>Mid-winter listings</b> (especially January or February) get less interest&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">You attract desperate or short-term tenants&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">You may feel pressured to lower rent or relax your screening criteria just to fill the unit&nbsp;</li>
</ul>
<h3>Strategic Timing = Higher ROI</h3>
The key is to <b>back-plan</b>. If your current tenant is moving out July 31, you should:
<ol>
 	<li aria-level="1">Begin marketing by early July (if not sooner)&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">Complete turnover work ASAP&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">Aim for a lease start date of August 1&nbsp;</li>
</ol>
This keeps your unit in the "sweet spot" for leasing and <b>minimizes costly gaps between tenants</b>.

RLPM uses data from Columbus leasing trends to time listings with precision. Our team also plans renewals and lease-end dates to line up with high-demand seasons whenever possible,  protecting your cash flow and improving your rental's performance long-term.

<h2>Your Reputation Matters: Why Responsiveness Drives Tenant Quality</h2>
Great tenants don't just look at the property,  they're also sizing up the person (or company) managing it.
<h3>Online Reviews Speak Louder Than You Think</h3>
Before filling out an application, many renters check <b>Google reviews</b>, read testimonials, or browse Reddit threads about local landlords and PMs.

If your reputation includes:
<ul>
 	<li aria-level="1">Complaints about slow repairs&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">Poor communication&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">Disorganized move-ins or unclear lease terms&nbsp;</li>
</ul>
…you'll turn away the kind of renters who care about professionalism,  the same renters who pay on time, take care of the home, and stay longer.

On the flip side, <b>positive reviews build trust</b> before you've even spoken.
<h3>Responsiveness = Professionalism</h3>
The rental process moves fast. If a qualified prospect inquires and you take 3+ days to respond, you've probably lost them,  especially in a competitive market.

Here's how slow responses hurt your leasing success:
<ul>

 	<li aria-level="1">You may end up settling for less-qualified renters who had fewer options&nbsp;</li>
 	<li aria-level="1">It signals that ongoing communication (for maintenance, billing, etc.) may also be poor&nbsp;</li>
</ul>
