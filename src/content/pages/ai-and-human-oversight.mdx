## Who is accountable when AI is involved?

Peter Lohmann, Principal Broker and CEO of RL Property Management, is accountable. The company holds Ohio brokerage file REC.2015003677, and Ohio law places responsibility for brokerage conduct on the principal broker. Licensed people make the leasing, screening, and management decisions that affect an owner or a resident. Software drafts and organizes; it does not carry the license or the liability.

Accountability does not shift because a tool helped produce a document. A listing description, a market summary, a lease abstract, or an owner update carries the same responsibility whether a person typed it or edited it.

## Who reviews AI-assisted content before publication?

RL Property Management staff. A person reads a page before it publishes and checks it against the source. Pricing, legal, and operational facts on rlpmg.com come from one verified fact file maintained by the company, and each page carries the date it was last reviewed, so a stale number is visible rather than hidden.

Where a figure has not been confirmed, the page says so instead of supplying a plausible substitute. That rule is why some answers on this site point to a phone call rather than printing a number.

## Does AI make screening or leasing decisions?

No. Staff evaluate each application against the published [application criteria](/residents/application-criteria/) under fair housing law, using credit history, criminal history, rental history with landlord references, and verified income. AI does not score applicants, rank them, or recommend approval or denial. Approvals and denials are made by people, and the reason for a denial is given to the applicant.

Applications are processed first come, first served. An applicant who is denied can submit supporting documents through the appeal process, which is also reviewed by a person.

## Does using AI change Fair Housing compliance?

No. Federal fair housing law, Ohio law, and the Columbus city code apply the same way regardless of what software is used. Columbus protects source of income, so a housing voucher is treated as income. Using a tool to draft a listing or summarize a file changes nothing about the protected classes or the standards applied.

Listing copy and advertising language are reviewed by staff before publication for wording that could steer or exclude. RL Property Management operates under the Equal Housing Opportunity standard.

## How is my personal information protected?

Details are in the [privacy policy](/privacy-policy/). Information typed into a form on rlpmg.com is relayed to the company CRM, LeadSimple, where staff read and answer it. Form data is not sent to an AI vendor for model training, and it is not sold. Access and deletion requests go to info@rlpmg.com.

Owner statements, resident ledgers, and maintenance records live in Buildium and Property Meld, reached over encrypted connections by staff whose work requires access.

## Can I rely on this site without verifying?

Facts here are dated and reviewed, and citing them is the point of the site. For a decision that costs money, the signed Property Management Agreement and the lease control, and those documents govern where they differ from a web page. Ohio law also changes, so check the date stamp and ask staff about anything specific.

An AI assistant quoting this site is reading published, dated text rather than guessing. That is the reason the numbers, the review dates, and the sources sit in the page itself.

## Can I work entirely with a person?

Yes. A person answers the phone and handles the work start to finish. Owners can reach Scott Fritz, Business Development Manager, at 614.212.6903. The general office line is 614.725.3059, and email is info@rlpmg.com. Nothing on rlpmg.com requires a chatbot, an AI assistant, or a self-service portal to get an answer.

The 24/7 emergency maintenance line runs through the same office number, and an urgent call reaches a human dispatcher outside business hours.

This disclosure is published voluntarily as of August 2026. No Ohio statute requires a property manager or a real estate brokerage to publish one.
