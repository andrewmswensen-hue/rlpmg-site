## What does flat-fee property management cost in a year with one lease signing?

Between $1,769 and $1,769. The flat fee does not move with rent, so the RL Property Management Standard plan costs the same on a $1,200 rental as on a $2,800 rental: $137 per month for 12 months plus the $125 annual accounting and technology fee. The market-standard column moves with rent, and the leasing fee equal to one month's rent lands in year one.

The arithmetic is short enough to check by hand. At $1,800 rent: 8 percent is $144 per month, or $1,728 for the year, plus a $1,800 leasing fee, for $3,528. The Standard plan is $137 times 12, or $1,644, plus $125, for $1,769.

## What do the same properties cost in a year with no lease signing?

A steady-state year flips part of the picture. With a resident renewing rather than moving, the market-standard model bills only the percentage, while the RL Property Management total adds the $250 lease renewal fee to the $1,644 in monthly fees and the $125 annual accounting and technology fee, reaching $2,019. Percentage pricing wins at the low end of the rent range.

Most properties see both kinds of year. Across a leasing year plus a steady-state year at $1,500 rent, the Standard plan totals $3,788 against $4,380 at 8 percent and $5,100 at 10 percent, a two-year difference of $592 to $1,312 in the owner's favor. Lease renewal rate across the RL Property Management portfolio was 72 percent as of July 2026.

## What assumptions sit behind these numbers?

Five, stated plainly. Rent holds flat for the year. Rent is collected in 12 of 12 months. Year one includes one lease signing and no renewal; the steady-state year includes one renewal and no lease signing. The percentage column charges 8 or 10 percent of collected rent. The RL Property Management column uses the Standard plan at $137 per unit per month.

Vacancy is the assumption that flatters the percentage column. A percentage manager bills nothing on an empty month, so real percentage totals run lower than shown whenever a unit sits empty. RL Property Management reimburses the monthly fee on a unit vacant longer than 60 days, and median days on market was 11 days as of July 2026.

## At what rent does a flat fee stop being cheaper?

In a leasing year, at roughly $900 per month in rent against an 8 percent manager and roughly $804 against a 10 percent manager. Below that, the percentage model costs less. In a steady-state year with a renewal, the crossover moves up to roughly $2,103 in rent against an 8 percent manager and roughly $1,683 against a 10 percent manager.

Average rent across the RL Property Management portfolio was $1,653 per month as of July 2026, which sits above both leasing-year crossovers and below the steady-state crossover at 8 percent. That is the honest shape of flat pricing: it wins decisively in years with turnover, and it trades close in quiet years at mid-market rents.

## How do the Passive and Premium plans change the totals?

By $240 and $564 per year against Standard. Passive at $117 per month totals $1,529 in a leasing year and $1,779 in a steady-state year with a renewal. Premium at $184 per month totals $2,333 and $2,583. The difference between plans is repair autonomy, not service scope: approval limits run $1,500, $750, and $350 respectively.

Premium also adds pre-listing support and use of the owner's preferred vendors. Owners with 5 to 9 units take 10 percent off the monthly fee per unit, and 10 or more units move to custom pricing, which shifts the flat column down again.

## What sits outside both columns?

Repair and maintenance costs, which an owner pays under either model. On the RL Property Management side, that means the 15 percent project management fee on third-party vendor work up to $3,000, capped at $450 per project, and in-house labor at $84 per hour plus a $15 trip charge. The one-time $300 onboarding fee on a vacant unit sits outside too.

Listing photography also sits outside: billed at vendor cost plus markup, typically $250 to $450, effective October 1, 2026, with existing professional photos on file used at no charge. Comparable markups and turn fees in the market-standard model vary by company and are not shown.

Figures above reflect RL Property Management pricing as of August 2026 and the market-standard Columbus model of 8 to 10 percent of rent plus a one-month leasing fee.
