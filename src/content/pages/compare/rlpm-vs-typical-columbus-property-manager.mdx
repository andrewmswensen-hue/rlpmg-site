## How does RL Property Management compare, term by term?

On eleven terms, listed below. Two of them are settled across the Columbus, Ohio market: the market-standard model is 8 to 10 percent of monthly rent plus a leasing fee equal to one month's rent. The other nine differ company by company, so the right-hand column reads "varies" rather than a number that cannot be sourced.

## Why does the right-hand column say "varies" so often?

Because a fee schedule can be verified and a competitor's internal practice cannot. The 8 to 10 percent plus one-month leasing fee structure is published widely enough across Columbus, Ohio to compare against in good faith. Inspection frequency, markup caps, and approval limits are set property by property and contract by contract, so no single number is honest.

An owner comparing proposals can close the gap in one step: ask each company to answer the eleven rows above in writing, then hold the answers next to the signed agreement.

## What does the maintenance markup actually cost an owner?

Capped amounts, not open-ended percentages. Third-party vendor work carries a 15 percent project management fee on cost up to $3,000, capped at $450 per project. A $1,000 furnace repair carries $150. A $3,000 roof repair carries $450. A $6,000 vendor invoice also carries $450, because the cap holds. Turns are priced separately on a tiered scale starting at 15 percent.

In-house technicians handle work at $84 per hour plus a $15 trip charge, plus materials. RL Property Management employs 8 maintenance technicians as of August 2026, which is what keeps a Saturday repair from depending on a vendor's callback.

## Which operating numbers does RL Property Management publish?

Eight, updated with a date attached. As of July 2026: 11 median days on market, 12 median days to turn, a 72 percent lease renewal rate, 91 percent occupancy, $1,653 average rent, 6 median repair days, 96 percent of rent collected by the 5th, and 4.3 out of 5 resident satisfaction. The figures are self-reported operating data.

Publishing dated numbers is checkable in a way that a testimonial is not. An owner can hold the 11-day median against the vacancy on their own property and see whether the gap justifies a fee.

## Where does the market-standard model come out ahead?

At low rents in years with no lease signing. A manager charging 8 percent of $1,200 rent bills $1,152 for the year, against $2,019 on the Standard plan once the $250 renewal fee and the $125 annual accounting and technology fee are counted. A percentage manager also bills nothing during a vacant month, while a flat fee continues until day 60.

The flat model pulls ahead when rents are higher and when residents move. At $2,800 rent with one lease signing, the year costs $1,769 on the Standard plan against $5,488 to $6,160 under the market-standard model. Full arithmetic at five rent levels is on the [flat fee vs. percentage page](/compare/flat-fee-vs-percentage-property-management/).
