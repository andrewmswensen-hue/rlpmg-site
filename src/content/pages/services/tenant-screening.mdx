## What does RL Property Management check on a rental application?

Screening at RL Property Management covers four categories: credit history, criminal history, rental history including landlord references, and income verification by pay stub or bank statement. Each category carries a published standard, and the same standard is applied to each applicant on a Columbus, Ohio rental. The four checks run together, not in sequence, so one weak area gets weighed against the rest of the file.

## How do criminal history and past evictions affect an application?

A violent criminal conviction disqualifies an applicant. An eviction filed against the applicant within the past {leasing.qualification.noEvictionsWithinYears} years also disqualifies the application, a lookback window that reaches back to 2021 for anyone applying in August 2026. Credit is judged on pattern rather than a single score: serious or recurring credit problems fail, while an isolated old collection generally does not.

The reason the standards sit in public view is that a Columbus, Ohio owner deciding between managers can compare them directly, and a resident who is declined can see the rule that applied. Consistency is also the practical defense in a fair housing complaint, since the standard, not the applicant's protected characteristics, drives the outcome.

## How much security deposit does RL Property Management collect?

Deposits run {leasing.securityDeposit}. A strong credit file lands at one month; a thinner or damaged file lands closer to two. Tying the deposit to credit lets RL Property Management approve a solid Columbus, Ohio applicant whose credit is imperfect without exposing the owner, instead of declining the applicant outright and leaving the unit vacant longer.

## What protects a Columbus rental against pet damage?

Approved pet households carry {usd(leasing.petDamageCoverage.amount)} of pet damage coverage, in place since {leasing.petDamageCoverage.since}. The coverage is funded by {leasing.petDamageCoverage.fundedBy.charAt(0).toLowerCase() + leasing.petDamageCoverage.fundedBy.slice(1)}, so the protection costs the owner nothing. Pets are screened alongside the applicant, and the owner sets whether the property accepts pets at all.

Pet-friendly listings draw a materially larger applicant pool in the Columbus market, and the {usd(leasing.petDamageCoverage.amount)} coverage exists so an owner can open the door to that pool without carrying the downside personally.

## How often does a screened placement end in eviction or a dispute?

{leasing.escalationRate.percent} percent of RL Property Management rentals escalate to eviction, litigation, or significant dispute. That figure is published rather than a screening promise: screening reduces risk and does not remove it. When a tenancy does go sideways, pay-and-stay arrangements and other alternatives are pursued before a filing in Franklin County Municipal Court.

Placement quality is prioritized over placement speed. A unit that sits an extra week for a stronger applicant costs less than a resident who stops paying in month five, because an eviction in Franklin County runs about {usd(evictions.costs.totalPossessionOnlyApprox.min)} to {usd(evictions.costs.totalPossessionOnlyApprox.max)} plus the vacancy and turn behind it.

## Is tenant screening billed separately to the owner?

No. Tenant screening (credit and criminal) is part of the flat monthly management fee on all three RL Property Management plans: {usd(pricing.plans[0].monthlyFee)}, {usd(pricing.plans[1].monthlyFee)}, or {usd(pricing.plans[2].monthlyFee)} per unit per month as of {pricing.effectiveLabel}. There is no per-applicant screening charge to the owner and no leasing fee when the placement is made.

A typical Columbus property manager charges {pricing.marketStandard.percentOfRentLow} to {pricing.marketStandard.percentOfRentHigh} percent of monthly rent plus a leasing fee equal to one month's rent each time a unit is leased, which means screening and placement cost more each time a resident turns over. The flat structure at RL Property Management removes that incentive to churn residents.
