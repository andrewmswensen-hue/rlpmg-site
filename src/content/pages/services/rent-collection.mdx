## How does RL Property Management collect rent in Columbus, Ohio?

Residents pay through an online resident portal rather than by mailed check, and the payment posts against the ledger for that unit automatically. Collection, follow-up on late balances, and the ledger itself are handled by RL Property Management staff in Gahanna, Ohio. {onTime} percent of rent across the portfolio was collected by the 5th of the month as of {kpis.asOfLabel}.

Owners see the same ledger from the other side, in the owner portal, so a payment that lands on the 3rd is visible without a phone call. The resident portal and the owner portal are both included on the Passive, Standard, and Premium plans.

## Why does screening show up in the collection numbers?

Because collections are decided before a resident moves in. Applicants must document income of {leasing.qualification.incomeMultiple} times the monthly rent, carry no violent criminal convictions, and have no eviction filed within the past {leasing.qualification.noEvictionsWithinYears} years. That front-end standard is what produces {onTime} percent collected by the 5th and a {leasing.escalationRate.percent} percent escalation rate as of {kpis.asOfLabel}.

Retention works the same way. The RL Property Management lease renewal rate was {kpis.metrics.find((m) => m.id === 'renewal-rate').value} percent as of {kpis.asOfLabel}, and a resident who renews is a resident whose payment history is already known. Placement quality is prioritized over placement speed for exactly this reason: the cost of a weak placement shows up in the rent ledger months later, not on the day the lease is signed.

## Does a flat fee change what rent collection costs an owner?

Yes, and the gap widens as rent rises. A typical Columbus property manager charges {pricing.marketStandard.percentOfRentLow} to {pricing.marketStandard.percentOfRentHigh} percent of monthly rent, which at {usd(avgRent)} per month is {usd(avgRent * pricing.marketStandard.percentOfRentLow / 100)} to {usd(avgRent * pricing.marketStandard.percentOfRentHigh / 100)} per unit per month. The RL Property Management {standard.name} plan is {usd(standard.monthlyFee)} flat, so a rent increase raises owner income instead of the management bill.

The same logic applies to turnover. The market-standard model adds a leasing fee equal to one month's rent each time a unit is leased. RL Property Management charges no leasing fee, and the monthly fee on a vacant unit is reimbursed if the property stays vacant longer than 60 days.
