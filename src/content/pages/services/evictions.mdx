## How long does a Franklin County eviction take?

About {evictions.typicalDurationWeeks} weeks in {evictions.court} as of {monthYear(evictions.costs.asOf)}, measured from filing to resolution. Court calendars, service of process, and any response filed by the resident can push the timeline out. Ohio eviction procedure is set by statute and by local court rules, and no property manager controls the docket.

The direct cost of the filing is the smaller number. The real cost to a Columbus, Ohio owner is the unpaid rent during those weeks, plus the vacancy and turn that follow, which is why alternatives get pursued first.

## Who files the eviction?

{evictions.handledBy} file and argue the case, not RL Property Management staff. Property management is a brokerage activity in Ohio, and courtroom representation is a legal one, so the two stay separate. RL Property Management assembles the ledger, the lease, the notices, and the property access, coordinates with the attorney, and charges a {usd(evictions.costs.rlpmProcessFee)} process fee for that work.

Using attorneys who file in {evictions.court} regularly matters more than it sounds. A defective notice or a missing ledger entry restarts the clock, and a restart costs another {evictions.typicalDurationWeeks} weeks of unpaid occupancy.

## What gets tried before a filing?

{evictions.alternatives} A payment plan that brings a resident current returns more to a Columbus, Ohio owner than a judgment against someone with no assets to collect against. RL Property Management works the balance first, and the file moves to an attorney when the arrangement fails or the resident stops responding.

Filing also has downstream cost beyond the {usd(evictions.costs.totalPossessionOnlyApprox.min)}: the unit sits empty during the case, the turn follows, and the lease-up follows that, averaging {leasing.averageLeaseUpWeeks.min} to {leasing.averageLeaseUpWeeks.max} weeks once the unit is rent-ready.
